# Logo Design Principles
A logo is the visual cornerstone of a brand identity. A good one is simple, memorable, timeless, versatile and appropriate. It is a symbol of the brand, not the brand itself: meaning comes from what the product does.

## The five qualities of a good logo
- Simple: minimal elements, no unnecessary detail. Test: can it be sketched from memory after one look?
- Memorable: a distinctive shape or idea, not derived from competitors
- Timeless: classic forms over fashionable effects; aim for a ten-year lifespan
- Versatile: works in one color, on light and dark backgrounds, from a 16-pixel favicon to a billboard
- Appropriate: matches the industry and personality. Finance reads as professional and trustworthy, creative work as expressive, wellness as calm and organic

## The five logo types
- Wordmark: the name alone in distinctive typography. Best for short, strong names.
- Lettermark: initials or a monogram. Best for long names and professional services.
- Symbol: a graphic without text. Best for brands that are already recognized; hard to build recognition with at first.
- Combination mark: symbol and wordmark together. Best for startups, since the two can later be used apart.
- Emblem: text inside a badge or seal. Best for traditional and heritage brands; less legible at small sizes.

## Color psychology for logos
- Red: energy, passion, urgency. Common in food, entertainment and retail.
- Orange: friendly, energetic, affordable, creative.
- Yellow: optimism, warmth, clarity.
- Green: growth, health, nature, sustainability. Common in wellness and organic food.
- Blue: trust, stability, professionalism, calm. Common in finance, technology and healthcare.
- Purple: luxury, creativity, wisdom.
- Black: sophistication, power, elegance. Common in fashion and premium goods.
- White: simplicity, cleanliness, minimalism.
Limit a logo to one or two colors, three at most.

## Typography in logos
- Serif: traditional, elegant, authoritative. Suits law, finance, publishing and heritage brands.
- Sans-serif: modern, clean, approachable. Suits technology, startups and contemporary brands.
- Script: personal, elegant, handmade. Often hard to read at small sizes.
- Display: distinctive, bold, attention-grabbing. Use sparingly.
Legibility comes before style.

## Shape language in logos
- Circles and curves: community, wholeness, continuity. They feel friendly and approachable.
- Squares and rectangles: stability, reliability, strength. They feel professional and solid.
- Triangles and angles: power, energy, direction. They feel dynamic and bold.
- Organic shapes: nature, originality, authenticity. They feel human and unique.

## Common logo mistakes
- Too complex: fine detail disappears at small sizes
- Too trendy: gradients, shadows and 3D effects date quickly
- Generic symbols: light bulbs for ideas, globes for international
- Unreadable typography: over-stylized lettering
- Too many colors: hard to reproduce consistently

## Writing a logo prompt for an image model
- Name the logo type first: "combination mark logo", "minimalist wordmark", "abstract symbol logo"
- Put the exact brand name in double quotes, such as text "EMBER", so the model renders the letters
- Describe one visual element concretely: its shape, what it depicts, how it relates to the name
- Name specific colors, two at most
- State the style in two or three words: "bold and geometric", "clean and modern", "elegant and organic"
- Ask for simplicity: "flat vector illustration, clean lines, high contrast"
- Set the presentation: "isolated on white background, centered"
