# 3D Rendering Techniques: Visual Style Guide for Image Generation Models

This document outlines various 3D rendering techniques, camera angles, lighting approaches, and stylistic elements for generating diverse visualizations of furnished floor plans. It guides image generation models in producing consistent and artistically appropriate 3D sketches.

## I. Rendering Styles

-   **Sketchy Architectural Drawing:**
    -   **Description:** Simulates the look of a hand-drawn sketch, often with imperfect lines, slight variations in line weight, and a somewhat loose, artistic feel. Focuses on conveying concept rather than photorealism.
    -   **Keywords:** `hand-drawn architectural sketch`, `loose line art render`, `sketchy ink drawing`, `concept art style`, `monochromatic sketch`
-   **Minimalist Line Art Render:**
    -   **Description:** Features clean, precise lines with minimal shading and flat colors, emphasizing geometric forms and clarity. Often uses a limited color palette.
    -   **Keywords:** `minimalist line art`, `clean architectural drawing`, `flat shaded render`, `geometric precision`
-   **Watercolor Architectural Render:**
    -   **Description:** Simulates watercolor painting, with soft, blended colors, subtle textures, and a dreamlike quality.
    -   **Keywords:** `watercolor painting style`, `soft blended colors`, `architectural wash render`, `artistic interpretation`
-   **Photorealistic Interior Render:**
    -   **Description:** Aims for maximum realism, simulating real-world lighting, materials, and textures. High detail in furniture, fabrics, and surfaces.
    -   **Keywords:** `photorealistic interior`, `realistic 3D render`, `architectural visualization`, `high fidelity materials`
-   **Isometric/Axonometric Diagram:**
    -   **Description:** A technical drawing style where objects are shown in a 3D perspective without foreshortening, maintaining parallel lines. Excellent for clear spatial representation.
    -   **Keywords:** `isometric view`, `axonometric projection`, `technical diagram`, `parallel lines no perspective distortion`

## II. Camera Angles

-   **3/4 Top-Down (Axonometric/Isometric):**
    -   **Description:** A common view for floor plans, showing the entire space from a slightly elevated, angled perspective. Provides a good overview of the layout and furniture.
    -   **Keywords:** `3/4 top-down view`, `axonometric camera angle`, `isometric interior shot`, `elevated plan view`
-   **Eye-Level Interior Shot:**
    -   **Description:** Simulates a human perspective from within a room, focusing on a specific area or feature. Good for conveying ambiance and detailed interior design.
    -   **Keywords:** `eye-level interior perspective`, `human height camera`, `room perspective shot`
-   **Top-Down Orthographic:**
    -   **Description:** A flat, bird's-eye view, similar to a traditional floor plan but with 3D elements. Good for clear layout and flow.
    -   **Keywords:** `top-down orthographic view`, `bird's-eye 3D plan`, `flat layout view`

## III. Lighting Approaches

-   **Soft Daylight:**
    -   **Description:** Simulates natural light from windows, providing soft, even illumination with gentle shadows.
    -   **Keywords:** `soft natural daylight`, `ambient light from windows`, `gentle shadows`
-   **Artificial Interior Lighting:**
    -   **Description:** Focuses on light cast by interior fixtures (lamps, recessed lights), creating specific moods or highlighting areas.
    -   **Keywords:** `warm interior lighting`, `directional spotlights`, `mood lighting`
-   **Ambient Global Illumination:**
    -   **Description:** Even, diffuse lighting from all directions, creating a soft, shadowless look.
    -   **Keywords:** `ambient global illumination`, `soft omni-directional light`, `shadowless interior`

---

**General Rendering Principles for Image Generation Models:**

-   **Consistency:** Maintain the chosen rendering style and camera angle throughout the visualization.
-   **Clarity:** Ensure architectural elements and furniture are clearly distinguishable.
-   **Scale:** Accurate representation of scale and proportion is crucial.
-   **Material Representation:** Materials (wood, fabric, metal) should reflect the chosen style and light conditions.
