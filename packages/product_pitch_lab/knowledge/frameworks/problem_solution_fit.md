# Problem-Solution Fit
Problem-Solution Fit is the first milestone of product development. It is reached when customers acknowledge a problem, are actively looking for a way out of it, and are willing to change their behavior to use your solution. It does not mean they will pay yet: that is Product-Market Fit, the next milestone.

## Problem validation 1: Is the problem real?
- Do customers mention the problem without being prompted?
- Do they already use workarounds or hacks?
- What does it cost them when the problem is left unsolved?
- Weak evidence: you describe the problem and they answer "I guess that is a problem"
- Strong evidence: they interrupt you with "Yes, I deal with this every day"

## Problem validation 2: Is the problem severe enough?
Rate severity on this scale:
- Critical: work stops without a solution
- High: significant pain, but workarounds exist
- Medium: annoying but tolerable, a "nice to have"
- Low: theoretical, rarely encountered
Only Critical and High problems reliably justify a new product.

## Problem validation 3: Is the problem frequent?
- Daily problems are stronger than weekly ones, weekly stronger than monthly
- Frequency drives both willingness to pay and retention
- An infrequent problem must be extremely severe to justify a solution

## Problem validation 4: Who owns the problem?
- Who feels the pain?
- Who controls the budget?
- Who makes the purchase decision?
In B2B the end user is often not the buyer, and the product must convince both.

## Solution validation 1: Does the solution solve the problem?
- Put a prototype in front of users and watch whether they use it unprompted
- Measure whether the problem shrinks or disappears
- Disappointment test: ask "How would you feel if you could no longer use this?" Very disappointed is strong fit, somewhat disappointed is moderate fit, not disappointed is no fit

## Solution validation 2: Is it 10x better than the alternatives?
A solution must be dramatically better, not incrementally better, to justify the cost of switching.
- What do people use today, including doing nothing?
- Why would they switch?
- What friction stands between them and adoption?

## Solution validation 3: Is it simple enough?
Complexity kills adoption.
- Can the value be understood in under 30 seconds?
- Can someone start without training?
- Does it fit into a workflow they already have?

## The five stages to Problem-Solution Fit
1. Problem discovery: interview 20 or more potential customers and look for patterns
2. Problem ranking: prioritize by severity × frequency
3. Solution hypothesis: define the minimal approach
4. Prototype testing: show a mockup and collect reactions to the concept
5. MVP validation: release the minimum viable version and measure engagement

## Strong and weak signals of fit
Strong signals are actions:
- Unprompted referrals ("you should talk to my colleague, she has this exact problem")
- Visible emotion when discussing the problem
- Returning to a prototype several times
- Trying to pay before being asked, or asking "when can I get this?"
Weak signals are words:
- Polite feedback ("that's interesting") and generic praise ("great idea")
- Future promises ("I'd probably use that")
- Comments about minor features, with no urgency

## Common mistakes on the way to fit
- Solution looking for a problem: something was built before the problem was validated. Talk to customers before building.
- Solving only your own problem: assuming everyone shares it. Check that it is widespread.
- Ignoring workarounds: if a tolerable workaround exists, only a dramatically better solution wins.
- Solving a low-priority problem: real, but not painful enough. Aim at the customer's top three problems.
- Confusing interest with intent: "I'd use that" is not usage. Measure behavior, not statements.
