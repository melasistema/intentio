# Architectural Design Principles: Functional & Aesthetic Guidelines for 3D Renders

This document outlines fundamental architectural and interior design principles that guide the intelligent furnishing and rendering of 3D floor plans. These principles ensure that generated visualizations are not only aesthetically pleasing but also functional, realistic, and compliant with common design standards.

## I. Functional Layout & Circulation

-   **Clear Pathways:** Ensure unobstructed and logical pathways between rooms and within a room (e.g., between furniture). Avoid blocking doors or windows.
    -   **Keywords:** `unobstructed circulation`, `clear walking path`, `functional layout`
-   **Zoning:** Group related activities or furniture into distinct zones (e.g., living zone, dining zone within an open-plan space).
    -   **Keywords:** `functional zoning`, `defined activity areas`
-   **Furniture Clearance:** Maintain appropriate clearances around furniture for ease of use and movement (e.g., space around dining table, in front of sofas).
    -   **Keywords:** `adequate clearance around furniture`, `proper spacing`

## II. Scale, Proportion & Balance

-   **Realistic Scale:** All furniture and architectural elements must be rendered at a realistic scale relative to the room dimensions.
    -   **Keywords:** `realistic scale`, `proportionally accurate`
-   **Proportionate Furniture:** Furniture items should be proportionate to each other and to the overall room size, avoiding oversized or undersized pieces that feel out of place.
    -   **Keywords:** `proportionate furnishing`, `balanced sizing`
-   **Visual Balance:** Achieve visual balance in room arrangements, considering weight, color, and form of objects. Both symmetrical and asymmetrical balance can be used effectively.
    -   **Keywords:** `visually balanced arrangement`, `symmetrical layout`, `asymmetrical balance`

## III. Light & Space

-   **Natural Light Optimization:** Furniture placement should not unnecessarily obstruct natural light from windows.
    -   **Keywords:** `optimize natural light`, `unobstructed windows`
-   **Sense of Spaciousness:** Layouts should promote a sense of openness and avoid clutter, even in smaller rooms.
    -   **Keywords:** `sense of spaciousness`, `uncluttered design`

## IV. Materiality & Cohesion

-   **Material Consistency:** Materials and finishes for walls, floors, and furniture should be consistent with the chosen overall rendering style (e.g., `modern`, `minimalist`).
    -   **Keywords:** `material cohesion`, `consistent finishes`
-   **Color Harmony:** Employ a harmonious color palette that complements the chosen style and user preferences.
    -   **Keywords:** `harmonious color palette`, `complementary tones`

## V. Functional Adherence

-   **Room-Specific Functionality:** Rooms should be furnished in a manner that supports their intended function (e.g., a bedroom must have a bed; a kitchen must have basic appliances).
    -   **Keywords:** `functional room setup`, `purpose-driven furnishing`

## VI. Negative Design Principles (What to Avoid)

-   **Clutter:** Avoid excessive or unnecessary furniture/decor that clutters the space.
-   **Obstructed Views:** Prevent furniture from blocking desirable views or focal points.
-   **Poor Circulation:** No furniture placed in such a way that it hinders movement.
-   **Unrealistic Proportions:** Avoid furniture that is too large or too small for the room.
-   **Stylistic Incoherence:** All elements should belong to the same overarching design style.
